\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_work}

Previous works and studies on the evaluation of broadcast algorithms for MANETs can be categorized in two classes: evaluations in the context of the proposal of new broadcast algorithms (\texttt{NBA} ), and performance comparison studies (\texttt{CS}).
%
Table~\ref{tbl:rw} summarizes the related work (\texttt{RW}).
%
The first column ``Study'' lists all the \texttt{RW} that we have
considered, tagged with either \texttt{NBA} or \texttt{CS} accordingly. For each \texttt{RW}, the column ``Many protocols'' shows whether the related work compares more than one protocol.
%
In the next column, we indicate if each \texttt{RW} has been compared to the simple flooding scheme (``Comp. to flooding'').
%
Further, the ``Many classes'' column indicates if the \texttt{RW}  is
compared either with other protocols that belongs to the same class or
not. 
%evaluated protocols belong to the same class.
%
Then, the columns ``Many densities'', ``Mobility'', and ``Energy''
highlight if each considered \texttt{RW} takes
into account respectively the impact of the network density, the
mobility and the energy consumption.

In their seminal work, Ni et al.~\cite{Ni:1999:BSP:313451.313525} identify the drawbacks of using basic flooding to perform broadcast.
%
The authors propose different algorithms that, by construction, outperform simple flooding.
%
Hence, they do not consider simple flooding in their evaluation.
%
\deleted{
Since by construction such approaches are an improvement, the authors then proceed to evaluate them without considering simple flooding.
}
This is a common pattern in evaluating broadcast algorithms.
% For that reason, it is important to use additional metrics, not considered in~\cite{Ni:1999:BSP:313451.313525}, to evaluate protocols.
The third column in Table~\ref{tbl:rw} shows that most of the studies avoid considering simple flooding on their evaluation.
%
However, as illustrated in~\cite{YiGerla2003}, comparing a broadcast algorithm
against the simple flooding is more than relevant as soon as metrics like mobility are taken into account.
%

Interestingly, most studies only consider comparisons among protocols
belonging to the same class.
%
For instance, authors only compare algorithms that build a CDS
in~\cite{Wu2004}, others authors only compare algorithms based on the
construction of cluster-based algorithms in~\cite{Nocetti2003}, and
finally, only probabilistic approaches with context awareness are
considered in~\cite{Cartigny:2003:BNR:2727141.2727459}.
%
Finally, in~\cite{Cagalj2002} the authors only compare algorithms that build a tree by varying the transmission range.

There are, nevertheless, some studies that compare protocols from different classes.
%
Previous work compare probabilistic and area-based algorithms~\cite{Ni:1999:BSP:313451.313525, Garbinato2010}; while some studies evaluate the construction of CDS-based and cluster-based topologies~\cite{Stojmenovic:2002:DSN:506156.506158, WCM:WCM109}.
%
% Given that few studies compare different categories of protocols, we believe that this is a limitation in the current state of the literature in MANETs. %% ER already said.
%
When considering power consumption, many studies do not measure the
cost of building and maintaining the underlying topology. They take
only in consideration the propagation of broadcast messages.

%
In fact, Table~\ref{tbl:rw} shows that most studies do not measure energy consumption at all.
%
Other variables such as mobility and how it affects the performance of algorithms are seldom evaluated.
