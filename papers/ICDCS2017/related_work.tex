\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_work}

Previous work on the evaluation of broadcast algorithms for MANETs can be categorized in two classes: evaluations in the context of the proposal of new broadcast algorithms (NBA), and performance comparison studies (CS).
%
Table~\ref{tbl:rw} summarizes the related work.
%
First column ``Many protocols'' shows whether the study compares more than one protocol.
%
Next, we look at how the details of this evaluation, starting with the comparison to the simple flooding scheme (``Comp. to flooding'').
%
The ``Many classes'' column indicates if the evaluated protocols belong to the same class.
%
Given that network density is extremely important in MANETs, we highlight in column ``Many densities'' the studies that consider this variable.
%
Then, two more variables are considered: mobility and energy consumption.
%
\deleted{
We show in Table~\ref{tbl:rw} whether a specific study takes into account the influence of mobility on the overall performance of the protocol and whether the impact on energy consumption is evaluated.
}
Finally, the last column shows whether the overhead on the communication medium was measured.
%
This variable matters as the amount of collisions and the performance of broadcast is directly linked to the amount of sent and received messages on the medium, including retransmissions.

In their seminal work, Ni et al.~\cite{Ni:1999:BSP:313451.313525} identify the drawbacks of using basic flooding to perform broadcast.
%
The authors propose different algorithms that, by construction, outperform simple flooding.
%
Hence, they do not consider simple flooding in their evaluation.
%
\deleted{
Since by construction such approaches are an improvement, the authors then proceed to evaluate them without considering simple flooding.
}
This is a common pattern in evaluating broadcast algorithms.
% For that reason, it is important to use additional metrics, not considered in~\cite{Ni:1999:BSP:313451.313525}, to evaluate protocols.
The third column in Table~\ref{tbl:rw} shows that most of the studies avoid considering simple flooding on their evaluation.
%
As shown in~\cite{YiGerla2003}, this approach to evaluation is a limitation because comparing against simple flooding is relevant when metrics like mobility are taken into account.
%
Interestingly, most studies only consider comparisons between protocols from the same class.
%
For instance, in~\cite{Wu2004} authors only compare algorithms that build a CDS.
%
Similarly, only the construction of cluster-based algorithms is evaluated in~\cite{Nocetti2003} and in~\cite{Cartigny:2003:BNR:2727141.2727459} only probabilistic approaches with context awareness are considered.
%
Finally, in~\cite{Cagalj2002} the authors only compare algorithms that build a tree by varying the transmission range.

There are, nevertheless, some studies that compare protocols from different classes.
%
Previous work compare probabilistic and area-based algorithms~\cite{Ni:1999:BSP:313451.313525, Garbinato2010}; while some studies evaluate the construction of CDS-based and cluster-based topologies~\cite{Stojmenovic:2002:DSN:506156.506158, WCM:WCM109}.
%
% Given that few studies compare different categories of protocols, we believe that this is a limitation in the current state of the literature in MANETs. %% ER already said.
%
When considering power consumption, many studies do not measure the cost of building and maintaining the underlying topology but only that of propagating messages.
%
In fact, Table~\ref{tbl:rw} shows that most studies do not measure energy consumption at all.
%
Other variables such as mobility and how it affects the performance of algorithms is seldom evaluated.
