\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_work}
%\er{Related Work should link to other studies of performance of protocols in MANETs. The related work on actual protocols should be covered in the previous section (Section~\ref{sec:background}).}
Previous studies in broadcast algorithms for MANETs are of two types: proposal and evaluation of new broadcast algorithms (NPE), and fully dedicated performance comparison studies (CS).
%
\deleted{
In this section, we discuss the evaluation criteria and the results obtained in a representative set of previous works.
}
In Table~\ref{tbl:rw} we summarize the related work.
%
First, in column \textit{Muliple protocols} we mark whether a study compares more than one protocol.
%
Next, we look how the evaluation take place.
%
That is, column \textit{Multiple classes} indicates if the evaluated protocols belog to the same classification.
%
Given that network density is extremely important in MANETs, we highlight in column \textit{Multiple densities} which studies consider this variable.
%
Finally, two more variables are considered mobility and energy consumption.
%
\deleted{
We show in Table~\ref{tbl:rw} whether a specific study takes into account the influence of mobility on the overall performance of the protocol and whether the impact on energy consumption is evaluated.
}
Additionally, the last column of Table~\ref{tbl:rw} shows whether the overhead on the communication medium is measured.
%
We believe this variable is important because in wireless communication, where collisions are a constant, the performance of a broadcast protocol varies depending on the number of sent and received messages.

In their seminal work, Ni et al.~\cite{Ni:1999:BSP:313451.313525} identify the drawbacks of using basic flooding schemes to perform broadcast.
%
The authors propose different algorithms that, by construction, outperform simple flooding and their evaluation is independent.
%
\deleted{
Since by construction such approaches are an improvement, the authors then proceed to evaluate them without considering simple flooding.
}
This is a common pattern in evaluating broadcast algorithms.
% For that reason, it is important to use additional metrics, not considered in~\cite{Ni:1999:BSP:313451.313525}, to evaluate protocols.
Third column in Table \ref{tbl:rw} shows that most of the studies avoid considering simple flooding on their evaluation.
%
As shown in~\cite{YiGerla2003}, this evaluation is limited because comparing against simple flooding is relevant when metrics like mobility is taken into account.
%
Interestingly, most of the studies only consider protocols of the same class.
%
For instance, in~\cite{Wu2004} authors only compare algorithms with a fixed transmission range that build one CDS.
%
Similarly, only the construction of \textit{cluster-based} algorithms is evaluated in~\cite{Nocetti2003}.
%
Meanwhile, in~\cite{Cartigny:2003:BNR:2727141.2727459} only \textit{probabilistic} approaches with \textit{context awareness} are considered.
%
Finally, in~\cite{Cagalj2002} the authors only compare algorithms that build a \textit{tree} by varying the transmission range.

%
Indeed, there are studies that compare protocols from different classes.
%
Previous work compare \textit{probabilistic} and \textit{area-based} algorithms~\cite{Ni:1999:BSP:313451.313525, Garbinato2010}; while some studies evaluate the construction of \textit{CDS-based} and \textit{cluster-based} topologies~\cite{Stojmenovic:2002:DSN:506156.506158, WCM:WCM109}.
%
Given that few studies compare different categories of protocols, we believe that this is a limitation in the current state of the literature in MANETs.
%
In terms of power consumption, many studies avoid to measure the cost of building and maintaining an underlying topology.
%
In fact, Table~\ref{tbl:rw} shows that most studies do not measure energy consumption.
%
Other variables such as mobility and how the latter decrease the performance of broadcast algorithms is barely evaluated, as it is shown in ~\cite{Ni:1999:BSP:313451.313525, Qayyum2002} where there is a probabilistic model to represent dropped packages at the MAC layer.
%
While this is a valid approximation, we think it is possible to better measure the effect of network overhead considering more realistic models of the MAC layer.
