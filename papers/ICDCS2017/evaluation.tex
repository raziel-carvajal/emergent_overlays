\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}
In Section~\ref{sec:scenarios} we describe the scenarios we use to evaluate the broadcast algorithms.
%
Now, we present the results of our experiments with and without mobility over different densities.
%
Such results are expressed in terms of the following metrics:

%
\noindent $\triangleright$
\textbf{Saved Rebroadcasts (SRE)} --
number of retransmissions we avoid by using an algorithm instead of \emph{simple flooding}\deleted{, which is considered the worst case}.
%
\deleted{To compute the SRE of one algorithm $A$ in a network of $N$ nodes, we measure the number $R_m$ of retransmissions made by $A$ to disseminate a message $m$. The $ \textit{SRE}_m$ for message $m$ is then $N - R_m$.}
%
\deleted{Since in our experiments we are disseminating many messages, we compute the mean \emph{SRE} and use it as metric for our comparison study.}
%
\deleted{This metric is useful to assess the performance of algorithms in terms of both network and energy usage. }

\noindent $\triangleright$
\textbf{Duplicate messages} --
number of times one broadcast message is received after its first reception.
%

\noindent $\triangleright$
\textbf{Reachability} --
percentage of nodes that successfully receive one broadcast message at least once.

\deleted{
\noindent $\triangleright$
\textbf{Broadcast time} --
time to disseminate one message with a reachability of 100\%.
}

% 
\noindent $\triangleright$
\textbf{Energy consumption} --
energy consumed by nodes during all broadcast session.
%

%\subsection{Network overhead}
\textbf{Network overhead}.
The proportion of SRE and duplicate messages determine to which extent the communication medium is saturated.
%
Figure~\ref{fig:a-saved} depicts the SRE in static scenarios where \emph{MPR} outperforms all other protocols.
%
However, the difference between \emph{MPR} and other algorithms decreases as the density increases.
%
Interestingly, \emph{ABBA} avoids retransmissions more efficiently than the \emph{CDS-based} technique for densities above 10. 
%
This pattern was expected in higher densities because ABBA cancels retransmissions and it outperforms \emph{CDS-based} by 10\%.
%
Figure~\ref{fig:b-saved} depicts dynamic scenarios, we observe that mobility affects the performance of topology-based algorithms.
%
Indeed, the build virtual topologies quickly becomes out of date due to the constant changes.
%
\deleted{Additionally, collisions of control messages in these algorithms is another explanation for the poor performance.}
%
\emph{ABBA} maintains its robustness but the cost of getting GPS coordinates is not considered.
%

The Proportion of Duplicate Messages (PDM) per algorithm is shown in Figures~\ref{fig:a-duplicated},~\ref{fig:b-duplicated} as a distribution with whishkers.
%
%As this value varies per each broadcast session, we present a distribution of the metric.
%
In general, the lower the PDM the better the utilisation of the medium. 
%
However, when PDM is compared against SRE the occurrence of collisions also affects message receptions.
%
For instance, using \emph{simple flooding} with density 30 nodes receive at most 10 duplicated messages instead of 30.
%
\deleted{In general, there are three reasons to get a low number of duplicate messages: i) a higher value of SRE; ii) the occurrence of collisions; and iii) broadcast messages reach few peers (low reachability).}
%
Our results in PDM confirm that \emph{MPR} avoids saturation in all scenarios.
%
\deleted{
Meanwhile, \emph{CDS-based} behaves similarly to \emph{ProbFlood} for low densities; but it outperforms \emph{ProbFlood} for density 20 and beyond.
}
%
\deleted{\emph{ProbFlood}'s behavior is specially poor in this metric, it is as bad as \emph{simple flooding} when the density is high.  }

\textbf{Power Consumption.}
\deleted{Power consumption depends on the number of messages exchanged.}
%
The amount of received and sent messages impacts power consumption due to the changes of mode the radio performs.
%
Thus, broadcast and control messages influence the energy consumption.
%
However, the network saturation in nodes vicinities influences energy consumption due to the way the MAC works.
%
In CSMA, nodes keep sending messages until receivers reply with an \emph{ACK}.
%
This strategy increases network reliability and energy consumption.
%
\deleted{
Since in such a case packets are often lost, CSMA keeps resending messages and consuming energy in the process.
}
