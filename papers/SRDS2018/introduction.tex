\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
The advent of the Internet of Things is leading to a revival of interest in mobile ad-hoc networks (MANETs).
%
A MANET is a mobile network that does not rely on a fixed infrastructure.
It is instead a self-configuring network.
%
Nodes connect directly to each other, and there is no guarantee for end-to-end connectivity.
%
Wireless topologies change frequently due to the mobility of its participants, there is no central entity in charge of routing, and most participants operate on battery-powered devices.
%
Applications based on MANETs~\cite{overview_adhoc_networks} range from sensor networks to end-user mobile applications, such as systems for traffic jam prevention or alert dissemination.
%

%%
Broadcast is a fundamental operation in MANETs, routing and group communication are examples of services that rely on this operation.
%
A vast amount of protocols have been proposed to figure out a trade-off between reachability and nodes resources (mainly energy consumption).
%
In~\cite{SurveyRuiz} broadcast protocols are classified in two groups. Protocols of the first group rely on overlays where nodes either forward incoming messages or act as receivers.
%
Nodes build and maintain an overlay via the periodic exchange of control packets that announce nodes existence.
%
The second group of broadcast protocols do not build overlays, instead, nodes forward messages until predefined thresholds reach a maximum value (e.x. number of receives copies) or the forwarding decision is based on a stochastic approach.
%
Reliable broadcast remains a challenging task, not only for deciding which is the protocol that shows better performance but also due to the operating conditions in MANETs.


%%
MANETs operate in dynamic scenarios where nodes move following patterns hard to predict and the interferences in transmission of packets are a constant.
%
We end up with a wireless heterogeneous network in terms of nodes density and the velocity at which nodes move within the area of communication.
%
This situation often takes place in real-life scenarios such as massive protest on the streets or in seminars with more than one speaker.
%

%
On the contrarily, a lower amount of points highlight more dynamic regions where participants tend to move faster, for instance, to transit between two points of interest (white gaps in figure~\ref{fig:manet-example:protest}).

Moreover, previous works on mobility models for wireless networks~\cite{bai2004mobility, ChaintreauA2006}, evaluation studies on broadcast strategies~\cite{DensityMobDrivenEval, graph-mob-model_Sholz2005} or micro-benchmarks on highly dynamic networks~\cite{adaptive_group_com_iscc02, ObraczkaK_2001} have shown that the performance of protocols for MANETs is influenced by operating conditions in the network, where mobility and density are key factors.


%%
We propose a self-adaptive protocol for MANETs in which nodes switch broadcast protocols according to local observations of the network conditions.
%
Nodes follow one main switching criterion that is triggered when the local perspective of nodes reaches predefined thresholds.
%
In our approach, overlays emerge in highly dense areas where nodes move slowly whereas controlled flooding techniques are deployed in dynamic areas with low density.
%
Dissemination overlays require nodes to keep track of their closest neighbors.
%
Dense areas with low velocity avoid abrupt changes in nodes surroundings, therefore, we find an opportunity for having more stable overlays that are easy to maintain.
%
Deploying controlled flooding techniques in more dynamic areas relies on previous studies on broadcast protocols~\cite{adaptive_group_com_iscc02} that have shown better performance. \raziel{add references to flooding approaches in sparse with low velocity of nodes}
%
Previous works on adaptive approaches for message dissemination in MANETs have shown that there is no one-fit-all solution~\cite{adaptive_group_com_iscc02}, we agree with this notion.\raziel{add references of adaptive approaches}.
%
Our adaptive approach, on the other hand, fills the gap in providing generalized solutions that integrate different broadcast techniques and deal with the interoperability issues of having multiple protocols running concurrently in a deployed MANET.
%
In concrete, our contributions are as follows:
\begin{itemize}
	\item A novel self-adaptive approach to broadcast in MANETs where nodes follows a main switching criterion, that is, the deployment of dissemination overlays in densely-populated areas whereas controlled flooding techniques are used in sparsely-populated areas.
	\item A list of adaptive policies mainly based on nodes velocity and density to trigger our switching criterion. Such list may be easily extended with other information available in nodes.
	\item To our knowledge, there is no previous work on broadcast protocols for MANETs that uses the local degree of nodes as a measure of density. We use the local clustering coefficient and a recently propose variation of it called closure coefficient~\cite{closure_coef} for the design of adaptation policies.
	\item Our adaptive approach is available to the research community in form of a framework, which uses Docker containers to help in the reproducibility of results~\cite{emergent_overlays_repo}.
\end{itemize}
