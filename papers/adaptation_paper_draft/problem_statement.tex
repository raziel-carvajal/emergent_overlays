Broadcast is a fundamental operation in Mobile Ad-Hoc Networks (MANETs)~\cite{SurveyRuiz}.
Besides allowing to dispatch a message to all nodes, broadcast is often the basis for routing mechanism~\cite{survey_routing}.

\emph{These decisions are based on the local knowledge that nodes collect on each message reception.}

Protocols of the second group use a pre-constructed overlay.
Nodes decide \emph{if} they must forward incoming messages based on their role in this overlay.
Typically, the overlay distinguishes between relay nodes that forward all incoming messages and passive receiver nodes that do not relay incoming messages.
The construction of the overlay is independent from broadcast operations.
This decouples the forwarding decision and the forwarding itself.

Selecting the most appropriate broadcast protocol for a given MANET deployment is a complex task.
Furthermore, the deployment conditions may change over time, hindering this decision-making.
Deployment conditions for a MANET include the characteristics of the individual nodes, such as their communication range and battery capacity, but also collective dynamics such as mobility and density in space.

A comparison study~\cite{DensityMobDrivenEval} shows that the best algorithm for a given situation, is generally not appropriate for a different situation.
A typical example is that for a high density network where nodes are mostly static, a protocol of the second class building an overlay is more efficient and robust, but that for a sparse network where nodes are highly mobile, protocols of the first class based on controlled flooding are better.

We are interested in designing an adaptive approach to broadcast in MANETs.
Adaptation refers to the ability of the system to change its behavior based on the observation of its deployment environment and of its performance.
In our target context, adaptation refers to the ability to dynamically select the protocol used for broadcast.
It is important to note that this adaptation and protocol selection should be possible not only for the entire system but also (and more interestingly) just for a part of it.
Indeed, the deployment conditions may not be homogeneous for all its nodes in a MANET .
Some nodes may for instance be in a densely-populated areas while other may be in sparsely-populated areas.
Nodes in some region may be very mobile while nodes in another region may be mostly static.
The best protocol for the nodes in one region may not be the best for the nodes in another region.
Nodes in one region should be able to group themselves and decide on the use of a different protocol.
This requires obviously mechanisms for the integration of this sub-system with the rest of the system, through interoperability mechanisms at the boundary between the two protocols.
