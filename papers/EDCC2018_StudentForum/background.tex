\section{Background}
A simple approach to broadcast is blind flooding where every node retransmit an incoming packet just once, further copies of previously received packets are ignored.
%
Blind flooding cause broadcast storms, term referring to a high rate of collisions and contentions in the network~\cite{Ni:1999:BSP:313451.313525}.
%
A vast amount of protocols have been proposed to deal with broadcast storms for finding an adequate trade-off between reachability and usage of nodes resources (mainly energy consumption).
%
P Ruiz and P Bouvry~\cite{Ruiz2015} classify \acl{bps} in two groups.
%
Protocols of the first group build and maintain an overlay by the periodic exchange of control messages that announce the existence of nodes.
%
Overlay-based approaches may form trees~\cite{796997}, clusters or connected-dominating sets (CDS), where every node is either port of a CDS or adjacent to at least one member of this set~\cite{Wu2001}.
%
In other words, CDS are forwarding sets and every other node out of it acts as receiver.
%
The second group of broadcast protocols do not rely on overlays, instead, the retransmission of packets take place following a stochastic approach~\cite{Kermarrec:2003} or when predefined thresholds reach their maximum value (number of copies of received packets, timers, number of neighbors, etc).


%
We compare our hybrid approach to the most representative protocols of each category presented in~\cite{Ruiz2015}.
%
Blind flooding is used as lower bound of performance, as an upper bound we use a centralized implementation of W. Matula algorithm~\cite{Matula:1987:DEC:1382440.1383039} to find an approximation of a CDS.
%
Our work is inspired from an evaluation study of \acl{bps} for \acs{nets}~\cite{7980187} driven by a plethora of configurations of mobility and density.
%
This study reveals that overlay-based approaches perform better in a dense-mobile network where nodes remain mostly static.
%
On the contrarily, in a sparse network where nodes move faster non overlay-based approaches show better results.
