\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

% We review related work on broadcast in MANETs, adaptation mechanisms in wireless and mobile networks, and studies of mobility in these systems.

A survey by Ruiz and Bouvry~\cite{ruiz2015survey} provides a comprehensive taxonomy of controlled flooding and overlay-based protocols, also covered in surveys by Reina \emph{et~al.}~\cite{Reina2015} and Santi~\cite{Santi:2005:TCW:1089733.1089736}.
%
% Broadcast protocols consider either fixed- or a variable-range communication~\cite{woon2011variable,park2013efficient}.
% Our focus in this paper is on fixed-range communication.
Our previous work~\cite{DensityMobDrivenEval} and the work of Williams and Camp~\cite{Williams2002} present comparative evaluations of broadcast protocols from different classes.
Hess \emph{et~al.}~\cite{Hess:2015:DHM:2856149.2840722} survey human mobility models for mobile networking.
Levy walk are a \emph{general} model~\cite{rhee2011levy}.
Other proposals allow modeling mobility in specific scenarios, such as disaster areas~\cite{Aschenbruck2009}.

The \emph{broadcast storm} problem associated with flooding is identified by Ni et al.~\cite{BroadcastStormProblem}, who also propose the \emph{controlled flooding} using counters and timers that we use in this paper.
The collection of topology information~\cite{ProbFlood} or GPS locations~\cite{abba} allows optimizing controlled flooding.
They are costlier and more sensitive to high mobility.

Overlay-based broadcast is based on the construction of connected dominating sets (CDS)~\cite{stojmenovic-cds}, with the goal of minimizing the number of necessary relay nodes.
MPR~\cite{mpr} or TSS~\cite{Nikolov2015} create CDS by guaranteeing the coverage of two-hop neighbors by at least one relay node.
Ahn and Lee~\cite{mpr-improvement} improve MPR to guarantee that each 2-hop neighbor is covered by $m$ such relays.
Other approaches~\cite{woon2011variable,park2013efficient} consider variable-range local broadcast to optimize the CDS.

Determining the optimal configuration for a MANET broadcast algorithm is difficult.
% Adaptation techniques have been proposed for different protocols and application contexts.
Kokuti and Vilmos~\cite{kokuti2012adaptive} propose adaptation techniques to dynamically decide on parameters in controlled flooding.
We described this approach in \S\ref{sec:evaluation} together with our other comparison point Hypergossiping~\cite{hypergossiping}.
\er{to change: we do no longer use hypergossiping as a comparison point but we use~\cite{adaptive_group_com_iscc02}}
Other approaches to adapt controlled flooding are based on measures of the nodes' velocity~\cite{adaptive_group_com_iscc02} or observed contention and collisions~\cite{2001:AAR:876878.879323}.
Liarokapis and Shahrabi~\cite{prob-based-adaptive-tech} propose adaptation techniques to switch from simple flooding to controlled flooding, and adapts the switching threshold to locally measured density.
Artimy~\cite{artimy2007local} proposes density estimation and dynamic range adaptation for vehicular networks, distinguishing between free-flow and congested phases.

The term \emph{emergent overlay} has been previously used in another context, wired unstructured P2P network~\cite{galuba2007generic}, to denote the \emph{on demand} construction of distributed hash tables~\cite{jelasity2009t}, following a centralized decision.
Bellavista \emph{et al.}~\cite{Bellavista2013} combine a static sensor network using an overlay and a MANET with controlled flooding for data collection for an IoT scenario, but the use of either protocol is determined in advance.
We are not aware of any previous approach combining in a single MANET controlled flooding and overlays.
