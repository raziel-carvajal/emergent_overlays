\begin{abstract}
Broadcast is a fundamental operation in Mobile Ad-Hoc Networks (MANETs).
%
A large variety of broadcast protocols have been proposed to deal with network dynamics and \emph{broadcast storms}, that is, the presence of collisions and contentions in MANETs as a consequence of the dissemination of messages.
%
Selecting the most appropriate broadcast protocol for a given deployment in MANETs is a complex task. 
%
Some characteristics of this deployment include the state of individual nodes, such as their communication range and battery capacity, but also the monitoring of network dynamics such as density in space.
%
We present an adaptive approach to broadcast in MANETs.
%
In our context, adaptation refers to the ability of a dynamic selection and configuration of the protocol used for broadcast.
%
This mechanism of adaptation and protocol selection is performed not only for the entire system but also, and more interestingly, just for a part of it.
%
We perform simulations and evaluate our approach using a mobility model that mimics how humans move to zones of interest, where overlays of nodes emerge as a result of applying our adaptive solution.
%
Our approach is presented as a framework between the network and application layers, to avoid interfering with the way each broadcast protocol operates.
%
Our experimental results reveal that network coverage remains high, while other broadcast metrics show a reasonable performance, in an heterogeneous MANET where more than one broadcast protocol is in execution.

\end{abstract}
