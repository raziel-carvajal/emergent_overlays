\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Table~\ref{tbl:rules} summarizes the main results of our experiments.
%
It has the form of a set of advices that allows us to chose a good broadcast algorithm for a given deployment scenario.
%
For instance, when there is no mobility, it is better to use \textit{MPR} no matter the density nor the frequency of messages because, as our experiments show, in such scenarios \textit{MPR} consumes less energy, guarantees good coverages and uses efficiently the medium.

The advices for deployment scenarios with mobility at walking speed are more complex, as they take into account other external parameters such as the density.
%
To suggest an algorithm, we first aim at increasing the coverage, then the energy consumption and we consider that the medium usage is the least important of the metrics.
%
For that reason, we propose \textit{simple flooding} when the density is very low.
%

Finally, the results show that \textit{ABBA} would be a good alternative when there is mobility and when the density is above 10.
%
Although for walking speed \textit{ABBA} is outperformed by CDS-based protocols, it is worth considering it as the main alternative for scenarios where there nodes move at higher speeds.
