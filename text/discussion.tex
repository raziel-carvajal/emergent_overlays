\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Table~\ref{tbl:rules} summarizes the main results of our experiments.
%
It has the form of a set of advices that allows us to chose a good broadcasting algorithm for a given deployment scenario.
%
For instance, when there is no mobility, it is better to use \textit{MPR} no matter the density nor the frequency of messages because, as our experiments show, in such scenarios \textit{MPR} consumes less energy, guarantees good coverages and uses efficiently the medium.
%

	\footnotesize
	\centering
	
	\begin{tabular}{lrp{1.3cm}|p{1.5cm}p{2.0cm}}
		\textbf{Mobility} & \textbf{Density} & \textbf{Frequency of broadcasts} & \textbf{Suggestion} & \textbf{Advices}\\
		
		\midrule
		Static & Any & Any & \textit{MPR} & Reduce frequency of control messages \\
		Walk Speed & 5 & Any & \textit{simple flooding} &  \\
		Walk Speed & 10 & Low & \textit{MPR} &  \\
		Walk Speed & 15+ & Any & \textit{node-degree} &  \\
		
	\end{tabular}
	
	\caption{Advices on what flooding algorithm to use in different deployment scenarios.}
	\label{tbl:rules}
\end{table}

The advices for deployment scenarios with mobility at walking speed are more complex, they take into account other external parameters such as the density and the frequency of broadcasts.
%
To suggest an algorithms, we first aim at increasing the coverage, then the energy consumption and we consider that the medium usage is the least important metric.
%
For that reason, we propose \textit{simple flooding} when the density is very low.
%
